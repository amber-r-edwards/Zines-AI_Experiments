Dear Sisters,

As you all know, one of the least logical prejudices of some people is that having a penis defines you. By the same token, removing that appendage automatically causes a most dramatic personality data loop that puts the fortunate non-owner in a new category free of sin.

So much for the intellectual.

Now for the personal.

I have lived for the past ten years as a woman. I am twenty-two.

This is my second letter to ITAB. The first should be somewhere in the archives of the Berkeley Post Office. It’s more lovey-dovey but less honest.

What was the effect of all the money and the surgery and the attention and the anxiety? What was it all supposed to be for anyway?

After it was over, I was still “me.” It did not transform me into anything. A vagina is just as ridiculous as a penis if you worship it.

This society does not value or recognize women. I changed my body because I was not comfortable being a man. I was always conscious of the fact that I was “feminine.” I did not believe them when they said “masculinity” was a virtue. John & I discussed the advantages of having a “pretty good game” but what good were men who had nothing else to offer?

When I was married to Ben, he wanted me to be a “real woman” so badly that I tried to make it happen for him, for me, for a lot of reasons. I was “feminine” but I was not a woman. It is possible to be a “feminine man” just as it is possible to be a “masculine woman.” But my body and my life were not related. I was unable to change my mind about myself. No amount of loving feelings or performing for someone else’s concept of me fixed that.

When the summer ended and the tenants left the apartment, I found myself alone again and faced with “the reality of me.” Van Morrison, Mary Wells, Ike & Tina Turner, The Rolling Stones, Motown all went off in a juke box memory, and the TV returned to my control. Once I made the decision the world looked everywhere but at me. I became a woman. I let hormones and surgeons and specialists build a new body for me.

I lived with John and Mary and Julia. They were the first lesbians I really got to know as friends, not
as “subjects.” They did not ever laugh at me, they trusted me because they liked me, and did not waste my time. It amazed me. After awhile, all of us became an “item.” I could never objectively guess who was the most important, and I think that’s the way it should be.

I still cannot bring myself to “relate” enough to get angry at the system. I guess I am what you call “soft.” I do get angry at men in groups, and at the stupid and at angry women but not at gay women.

I have tried to teach myself to relate to my body but it is not easy. Enough for now. Thank you for an opportunity for some therapy.

Love,
beth

OUR Response

So, that to change a man must exper ience being a woman, that is the answer, the only answer men have tried to under stand. It’s not enough, not at all enough. our experience tells us they still want to relate to their own images, and the operating experience doesn’t begin to approach anything about what being a woman is. It may be necessary for all men to have their testicles removed so that perhaps eventually, through repetition, an ambigu ous fear can be communicated; it is certainly not enough to merely be told something is dangerous. castration is necessary for the wish for rape, hostile act of childbearing occur s in those who do not want children.

Farthest along the road as of today, partial transvestites (full ones, presumably, are the ones who couldn’t go back at all) make partial attempts to behave as women, partially successful, that much is legitimate.

We have noticed what happens. There was (and still is) a very real split. Those who would exercise no privileges at all, no initiative, and those who would attack with disguised “self-dislike.” The first are more “trapped,” the second more “a man.” The hard parts, besides the realization that there are ways of behaving essential to feeling like a woman/man, are the parts of behavior that are related to op pressors or the oppressed.

Our conversation sent around like this: Is being a woman something you return to the group about, something you decorate the group about? This is just more of the famous “I’m taking my marbles and going home” stick. Men will do anything before they will truly give up their status gap.

But basically i don’t like my body.

Now I shouldn’t have to say this, but here: the only real discrimination in this country, the only legitimate one, is between men and women, oppressor and oppressed.
