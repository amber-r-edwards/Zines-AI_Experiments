He was freaked, but pro-ceeded to examine her and he was surprised (we weren't) to find that she did have cervicitis (any irritation of the cervix). So although we cannot diagnose or prescribe, we teach other awareness, and there is a second reason we believe doctors are threatened.  They need and want their elit-ist barrier in between patients and themselves and hold up technical information and jargon for that reason.  The kind of aware-ness that comes from knowing our own bodies is vital to our health.  They fear women having this knowledge and passing it on to other women.  It is power which we must take from them.  It's simply not supposed to be our jurisdiction to know or care about our own bodies.

This kind of paranoia clear-ly shows the current bust at the L.A. Feminist Women's Health Center.  Two women were arrested for teaching other women how to do self-exam.  It is incredible that other men could try to stop us from taking con-trol of what should naturally belong to us.

We have been questioning practices for generations (such as forced sterilization, unau-thorized hysterectomies, un-necessary prescriptions and hospitalizations), and when we have confidence and reliabil-ity in English or another method which directly corre-sponds with fact, they show their fear.  At the arrest, an L.A. detective said to one of the women, "I have a daughter I don't want you talking to."  We must give each other strength and knowledge to spread what we feel is neces-sary to do whatever is necessary to become whole.  These two sentences taken from the Feminist Women's Health Center sums it up beautifully.  The concept of self-help stresses SISTERHOOD that makes possible the bene-fits from collective know-ledge, collective exam-ining, collective re-thinking, especially the conversation before or after exam, when it is neces-sary to travel for more extensive work, for more sharing, service or help.  Lastly, the self-help con-cept makes certain the use of safe equipment at all times.
