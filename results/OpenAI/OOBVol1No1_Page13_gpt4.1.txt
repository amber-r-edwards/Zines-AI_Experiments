the diaphragm

by Marlene Wicks

This is a serial column written to serve as a forum for physical and mental well-being. The recent “pill” re­harnangs have stunned us all. We must turn to other means of birth control un­til we have more information regarding oral contraceptives. I’ll start with the diaphragm.
The diaphragm, also called a pes­sary, is a birth control device and the oldest in terms of the woman’s self using of all the devices we know. It is desig­ned as a feminine item, being of soft rubber and must be accompanied by a jelly made with water as a carrier. It is bell-shaped, and rubber and looks like half a ping pong ball. The rim is made of a coil spring covered with soft rubber, which makes the spring of a flat spring variety and will spring open to remain inside the vagina.
Many creams or jellies are avail­able. You should always use the cream or jelly with the device. Most jellies and creams contain nonoxynol-9, a sperm killer, and either one or the other is recommended for use with the device as the rubbert can become cracked and dry and the vagina irritated.
To insert, insert the diaphragm stand­ing up, squatting, or lying on your back as you would insert a tampon, using finger and thumb. Squeeze the device into a narrow, oblong shape (fold in half). Apply the jelly around the rim, using about two tablespoonfuls (an ap­plicator is included in the package for use in applying cream or jelly inside the dome of the diaphragm after it is in place). One-eighth teaspoon is enough. Like a curved plastic stick, fitting one rim into the end groove, then hooking the opposite rim over the appropriate notch. Easier than introducing on the floor of the vagina, as you would a tam­pon. Never leave the diaphragm in long­er than overnight. Once it is out and the diaphragm is in place, give it a quarter-turn left or right which disengages the pubic bone. Don’t use a lot of jelly, and the diaphragm is then pushed be­hind the pubic bone. Check as before to make sure she is covering the cervix.
When inserted properly, neither you nor your mate should be able to feel the device at all. When the manufacturer’s directions are followed, the use of the preparation is not harmful to the vaginal canal and not a cause of cancer.
The diaphragm is effective for from six to eight hours after intercourse. There should be repetition and use of reframed intercourse patterns which you may with to use in case of breakage. If you the diaphragm will help protect you.

psychology of the diaphragm
by Alison Sand

A diaphragm doctor told me that if using a diaphragm were a test of reliability “You’d have to test regularly—say once a year in order to make sure you remembered how to use it. Strangely enough, this is not true for other women…You have an emotional reaction about touching and inserting the diaphragm—one of revulsion and a sense of dirtiness—of not being able to face flesh and blood flesh and blood you.
To learn to use your diaphragm before you need to use it. Take the time for a hundred readings of the special parts of your vagina or uterus, to find out that the child inside you finds no difference from the outside than the outside than your own hand, nor is your reaction “not okay.” Practice outside and inside the room, practice until you are familiar, there is the chance of breakage and you are not going to hurt yourself, that is why repeated readings are so important to learning, to learning reminders, for your own flesh and blood.
Self-exploration is vital. Explore yourself carefully, with a mirror perhaps. Do not be afraid or disgusted. There is joy to be exploring your vagina in search of your cervix, in actually touching your cervix, and learning how to measure your vaginal opening so your diaphragm fits.
The outer parts of yourself you are so accustomed to seeing; the inner parts, not.
In some way you will find if you dig and search and prod into your differ­ences from others, you will become familiar with, and capable of using the diaphragm effectively without fear or disgust. It will become as familiar, as reassuring, and as nonthreatening as the use of a toothbrush, if that comparison will not offend your sensibilities.
It’s not any different than any other birth control and in only one way when it’s important it’s because it’s your flesh only, not his, which can help you.
It’s his interest and clearly not his concern, only yours to see to it, to keep this relationship in focus—that it is yours, for making with old flesh and blood you.
